Whilst 71% of the area will be retained as either green or blue space, the expanded Campus will be delivered with commercial-led objectives. Wellcome intend to retain ownership of all assets on completion and this allows the design/procurement/delivery/handover/maintenance/ management of all buildings to be driven by a holistic and standardised approach.

Urban&Civic was acquired by Wellcome Trust in 2021 and subsequently appointed as Development Manager to manage and deliver the development of the expansion under a Development Management Agreement. As part of this appointment Urban&Civic has sought to encourage and integrate a whole life approach to the development through engagement and consultation with stakeholders, designing in flexibility and innovation to the infrastructure and buildings to allow science and sustainability to flourish, whilst recognising the need to understand the estate and building management strategies following the completion of development.

Urban&Civic recognise the challenge of bringing forward a series of future-focussed sustainable buildings, incorporating both the latest science led technologies and innovative heating and cooling energy systems within environmentally sensitive and embodied carbon – lite structures. It therefore has sought to address this by taking the key stakeholders, namely Wellcome, their brokers and underwriters, on the journey through to completion of the development so all parties fully understand the risks and associated mitigation to be considered at each stage. The outputs would be led by the initial analysis of the key risks perceived by the stakeholder group.

Following initial dialogue with Wellcome and their broker, Marsh, the key and recognised risks of fire and water were confirmed as being relevant to the study. However, the matter of Estimated Maximum Loss (EML) was seen as the key driver due to the number of buildings being delivered across the expanded Campus.

The study therefore set about responding to these briefings by focussing on the delivery of two key documents;



–


Design Guide – aA design guide prepared by Arup that sets out a novel calculation methodology to assess and limit the fire spread risk from one building to another, taking into consideration the anticipated fire service attendance times at the new Campus. It has been developed in recognition of existing methods in the UK not adequately capturing the hazard. The new methodology is validated against the latest research in masstimber fire dynamics and fire safety of timber cladding. The guide also includes recommendations to

enhance resilience of suppression systems, and sets out tips to make building envelops and landscapes more resilient to wildfires. It is accompanied by illustrations by WTA of possible wall and roof construction methods that could be deployed on the site, and an example application of the external fire spread analysis..



–


Design Guide Examples – a series of worked examples, using the RISC Authority Insurance Evaluation Tool, to allow design teams to apply the Design Guide to any of the three building types selected and based upon the illustrative master plan for the Wellcome Genome Campus expansion. This would also consider reducing EML below 100% within buildings and potential solutions for this.

The work has progressed over a twelve month cycle and has included extensive stakeholder engagement with Wellcome, their broker and the design teams appointed to bring forward the initial buildings at the expanded Wellcome Genome Campus. The two documents are seen to be consultative rather then instructional. They may naturally evolve during the course of the development, led by changing market perceptions, technological advances, new user classes to be considered or a significant change to the footprint of a specific building.
